# What goes wrong: recurring clause types that burn people

Research method: DuckDuckGo (html.duckduckgo.com/html/) searches via Chrome DevTools MCP, following results to primary sources (Reddit threads, seller forums, consumer complaint sites, news outlets, law-firm blogs). No WebSearch/WebFetch tools used.

**Searches used: 11 / 12. Page reads: 2 / 15** (most evidence quotes were pulled directly from search-result snippets that already contained the sourced first-person text and exact URL; two pages were opened in full for additional context).

Note on source quality: a large share of DuckDuckGo results for these queries were SEO/content-farm "contract review" blogs (many with suspiciously on-the-nose domain names and 2026 publish dates) that describe clause dangers only in the abstract, with no real victim attached. Those were **excluded** from the findings below. Only findings with a traceable first-person account, named news event, or identifiable law-firm-documented pattern are included.

---

## Ranked findings (by volume/severity of evidence actually found)

### 1. Auto-renewal / cancellation-friction clauses
Most represented in the search results — showed up as a live consumer complaint pattern and as a sales-side admission that these terms are used adversarially.

- **r/sales, "60 day auto re-new contracts"** — a sales professional says requiring 60 days' notice to cancel is "indefensible and a tactic used by shit companies to unethically extract one more payment from unhappy customers," adding that "the more strict a company is about cancelling renewals the less financially stable they are." This is a practitioner confirming the mechanism is used deliberately, not just an accidental trap.
  https://www.reddit.com/r/sales/comments/1c4styg/60_day_auto_renew_contracts/

- **Avvo.com legal Q&A, "Gym kept charging after in-person cancellation"** — first-person complaint: "In October 2023, I called Orangetheory Fitness (Kingstowne, VA) to cancel my membership. I was told to cancel in person, which I did... over $1,900 taken without notice." A cancellation procedure buried in the contract (in-person only, specific form) converted an ordinary cancellation into over a year of unwanted billing.
  https://www.avvo.com/legal-answers/gym-kept-charging-after-in-person-cancellation-ove-6215431.html

### 2. Arbitration clauses / class-action waivers
Currently live, high-profile, and multiply corroborated across independent outlets in the same week.

- **Reuters, "Amazon reinstates binding arbitration, bars class-action lawsuits"** (Aug 14, 2026) — Amazon's new terms require U.S. customers to resolve disputes through individual arbitration rather than the courts, and bar class actions.
  https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/amazon-reinstates-binding-arbitration-bars-class-action-lawsuits-2026-08-14/

- **Daily Mail, "Amazon quietly BLOCKS customers from taking them to court"** — reports customers "furious" the change "took effect immediately... by continuing to use its services," with the class-action waiver also reinstated.
  https://www.dailymail.com/yourmoney/article-16064151/amazon-bans-customers-court-arbitration.html

### 3. Non-compete clauses
The strongest documented *harm severity* of any category — a specific dollar figure tied to a low-wage worker.

- **CBS News / FTC enforcement action** — FTC settled with three companies over noncompete agreements that "prevented employees from leaving for competitors."
  https://www.cbsnews.com/news/noncompete-agreement-feds-sue-3-companies-that-prevented-employees-from-leaving-for-competitors/

- **WSGR (law firm) summary of the same FTC action** — names the mechanism concretely: "Violation of this agreement would require the guards to pay Prudential $100,000 in penalties, even though Prudential's security guards typically earned hourly wages at or near minimum wage."
  https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-takes-action-against-employee-noncompete-clauses-through-rulemaking-and-enforcement-actions.html

### 4. Unilateral / no-notice termination clauses
Real first-party account of a long-standing business relationship ended overnight under a platform's terms.

- **Amazon Seller Central (Europe) forum, "Has Anyone Else Had Their Amazon Shipping Account Terminated Without..."** — seller writes: "We've just received a notice from Amazon Shipping informing us that our shipping services will be terminated as of 2 August 2025... We've been loyal customers of Amazon Shipping for over 7 years, and this came completely out of the blue. No explanation was provided, and our account manager wasn't informed either."
  https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/5b08aee2-2a56-4c3b-9732-ab87dcdc8ee7

### 5. IP assignment / exclusive-rights clauses
Recurring theme across multiple r/freelance and r/freelanceWriters threads — the harm here is professional (loss of portfolio/reputation-building material), not financial.

- **r/freelance, "Client wants exclusive rights to my work?"** — freelancer describes a standard portfolio-use clause in their own contract being challenged: "The client has pushed back saying that they must be the exclusive owner of the content and I cannot have rights to it whatsoever."
  https://www.reddit.com/r/freelance/comments/zab3yg/client_wants_exclusive_rights_to_my_work/

### 6. Indemnification / liability-shifting clauses
Weaker on named victims, but corroborated as a real, law-firm-observed pattern rather than a hypothetical risk.

- **Melchert Hubert Sjodin, PLLP (law firm blog), "The Hidden Danger in Your Contracts"** — describes the mechanism with a concrete scenario: a subcontractor who agreed to indemnify a general contractor for "any and all claims arising out of the project" can end up paying "medical bills, legal defense, and settlements" for an accident caused by the general contractor's own oversight, and states plainly: "Cases like this have bankrupted small operations overnight." Also notes standard liability insurance often excludes contractual indemnification obligations, so the exposure is uninsured.
  https://mhslaw.com/the-hidden-danger-in-your-contracts-indemnification-clauses-that-can-sink-a-small-business/

### 7. Fee / price-escalator clauses (weakest evidence found)
Confirmed as a recognized red-flag pattern in SaaS/vendor contracts, but no first-person "this actually happened to me and cost me $X" account turned up in the searches run — only advisory content describing the mechanism ("The vendor can unilaterally change the contract terms (prices...) with 30 days' notice... You're locked in for a year, but the vendor can change the deal mid-contract.") No independently-reported news story or forum complaint of a specific price-escalator harm was found within budget.
  https://www.getpricepulse.com/blog/saas-contract-red-flags-2026.html

## Not found / explicitly not confirmed

- **Liability caps** as a standalone category with a named-victim story: not found separately from indemnification within the search budget. The one dollar-figure anecdote surfaced ("We Thought It Was a 'Small Clause' — Until We Lost $53,000," talkcounsel.com) reads as marketing content for a legal-services blog rather than a verifiable independent account, so it was excluded rather than cited as evidence.
- **Fee/price escalators**: pattern confirmed as real and commonly flagged, but no sourced example of concrete harm to a specific party was found — see note in section 7 above.
- Searches for a landlord/rent-escalation-clause news story returned zero results and were not pursued further given the search budget.
