# What Already Exists — Contract Review / Clause Analysis Products

Research method: Chrome DevTools MCP browser only (DuckDuckGo `/html/` search + direct page reads).
Totals used: **5 searches, 4 page reads** (well under the 12 search / 15 read caps; stopped once 10 distinct sourced findings were collected across 4 products spanning legacy-consumer, legacy-SMB, AI-native-enterprise, and AI-native-consumer).

A note on search noise: the very first query surfaced a cluster of near-identical "free AI contract/lease reviewer" domains (freecontractreviewer.com, leaseai.net, justee.ai, revise.io, scrutr.ai, lexplio.com, readmycontract.ai, contractmaker.ai, arklegal.ai, contractofframp.com) with suspiciously templated marketing copy. These were not verified further (no independent pricing or review-site corroboration could be found for them within budget) and are **not** included as findings below — only products with corroborating detail from a second, independent source (official pricing page, Trustpilot, G2, FTC.gov, Lawyerist, ConsumerAdvocate, etc.) are listed.

---

## Rocket Lawyer (legacy, still adding AI features)

**What it does:** Online legal services platform — create/sign legal documents, get attorney consultations, business incorporation, and (via "Rocket Copilot") an AI feature that reads an uploaded contract and highlights key terms, flags potential risks, and suggests what needs a second look before signing.
Source: https://www.rocketlawyer.com/contract-review

**Who it targets:** Small business owners and individual consumers (leases, NDAs, independent contractor agreements, etc.) — testimonials on the page are all "Small Business Owner."
Source: https://www.rocketlawyer.com/contract-review

**Pricing:** Contract review via Rocket Copilot is advertised as FREE. Attorney "Legal Advice Session" (30/60 min) starts at $149; live 20-minute attorney calls and full membership benefits require a paid/trial membership.
Source: https://www.rocketlawyer.com/contract-review

**Most common complaint:** Overall sentiment is strongly positive (Trustpilot TrustScore 4.5/5 from 9,802 reviews, 81% 5-star, only 5% 1-star), but Trustpilot's own review-summary flags "Subscription" and "Cancellation" as the two categories with the most mixed/ambiguous customer experiences, and the site surfaces a dedicated "Cancellation" filter tag. A representative negative review: "Its take so long worked with manufacturing company not for you false promise every time law firm take fee from your money" (1-star).
Source: https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rocketlawyer.com

## LawDepot (legacy, consumer/SMB document prep)

**What it does:** Legal document preparation service (wills, leases, business contracts, etc.) — document templates and guided drafting rather than clause-level AI analysis.
Source: https://www.consumersadvocate.org/last-will-and-testament/c/law-depot-review

**Who it targets:** Individual consumers and small businesses needing legal documents without hiring a lawyer.
Source: https://www.consumersadvocate.org/last-will-and-testament/c/law-depot-review

**Pricing:** Operates on a membership/subscription model with a 7-day free trial; a document (e.g., a will) can be obtained free only if the trial is cancelled before it converts to a paid membership.
Source: https://onlinewillmakers.com/lawdepot-review/

**Most common complaint:** "A good number of customers have found that it's difficult to cancel their subscriptions after enrolling" — despite an otherwise strong 8.8 Trustpilot-based score (69%+ positive feedback).
Source: https://www.consumersadvocate.org/last-will-and-testament/c/law-depot-review

## Spellbook (AI-native, enterprise/law-firm legal teams)

**What it does:** AI platform for commercial legal work that lives inside Microsoft Word — runs first-pass contract reviews, suggests redlines, drafts clauses grounded in the user's own playbooks and market-standard language, and flags terms that deviate from house playbooks.
Source: https://lawyerist.com/reviews/artificial-intelligence-in-law-firms/spellbook-review-artificial-intelligence-for-lawyers/

**Who it targets:** Transactional lawyers, in-house legal teams, and law firms handling commercial agreements — explicitly NOT consumers, and explicitly "not ideal for" firms wanting simple published pricing or case-law research.
Source: https://lawyerist.com/reviews/artificial-intelligence-in-law-firms/spellbook-review-artificial-intelligence-for-lawyers/

**Pricing:** No published pricing — "Contact Vendor," with a 7-day free trial available. A third-party estimate elsewhere pegs it around "~$99/user/mo (quote-based)," but that figure is not confirmed on Spellbook's own site and is flagged here only as an unverified secondary claim.
Source: https://lawyerist.com/reviews/artificial-intelligence-in-law-firms/spellbook-review-artificial-intelligence-for-lawyers/

**Most common complaint:** Lawyerist's structured review lists "No Published Pricing" and "Add-on Product" (i.e., it's a Word plug-in, not a full contract lifecycle management platform) as the two "Cons." G2's user-review page for Spellbook could not be reached (blocked by a DataDome bot-verification challenge), so no first-person user-review complaint could be sourced within budget — noting this gap explicitly rather than guessing.
Source: https://lawyerist.com/reviews/artificial-intelligence-in-law-firms/spellbook-review-artificial-intelligence-for-lawyers/

## DoNotPay (AI-native, consumer)

**What it does:** Marketed itself as "the world's first robot lawyer" — an AI chatbot/subscription service claiming to handle consumer legal tasks (including document/contract-related help) without a human lawyer.
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/donotpay

**Who it targets:** Individual consumers.
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/donotpay

**Pricing:** Not captured in this research pass (page not visited within budget) — not stated here to avoid guessing.

**Most common complaint (regulatory, not a review-site complaint, but the single most consequential and well-sourced finding on this product):** The FTC brought a formal enforcement action (complaint filed September 2024, order finalized February 2025) alleging DoNotPay never tested whether its law-related features actually performed like a human lawyer, and that subscribers who used the law-related features complained the service didn't deliver on that promise. The case settled with a $193,000 fine, mandatory consumer notifications, and a prohibition on making unsubstantiated AI-capability claims.
Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires ; https://qz.com/ftc-ai-donotpay-scam-1851657725 ; https://airiskaware.com/enforcement/ftc-donotpay-2025
